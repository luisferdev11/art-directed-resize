What the template mechanism reportedon this same master

Verbatim text from their own tool. Neither their outputs nor screenshots of their product are reproduced: only what their own report says about itself.

I can see you have 2 templates, but none are landscape. I would suggest designing a landscape version for a smoother rollout.— before it runs
49 outputs were matched to your square template - including landscape and ultra-wide sizes like 1040x480, 1152x324 and 1280x384. These have the biggest stretch, so headlines, images and logos may sit off-centre or get cropped.— in the final report
17 outputs went to your portrait template - sizes like 320x736 and 512x768. These are closer but still off, so expect minor reflow issues.— in the final report
The clean fix is adding a landscape template (and ideally a couple more native ratios), then re-running. Otherwise you'll want to nudge the layouts on the wider sizes by hand.— the solution it proposes
And here one has to be fair. That mechanism was given two templates and not one of them landscape, and their tool warned about it before running. Presenting the result as a failure of the product would be dishonest: the product did exactly what it says it does.

That is not the argument. The argument is that the solution their own tool proposes is to author more templates, and that work scales with the diversity of formats, not with the number of campaigns. A set of 66 sizes has ratios that three templates do not cover, and their report says so: "ideally a couple more native ratios".

The same sizes, decided from the artthe three their report names, plus the extremes

The triage38 need nothing, 28 are flagged with their reason

The declared goal is a rough first pass, and the triage is what turns that from an apology into a feature: the designer knows which ones to open instead of reviewing all 66.

38no flags
19review: a somewhat heavy scrim or tight contrast headroom
9attention: insufficient PPI or the photo degraded to a panel

None of those flags is a structural failure: all 66 pass the validator. They are declared judgements about how much of the photograph is covered and how much headroom the contrast has. The per-role weights are in the code, in plain view. See the contact sheet with all 66 pieces →

What had to be decidedand was not written in any template

  • 1 formats degraded the photograph to a panel because no bleed crop contained the subject above its minimum scale. It is a structural decision that emerged from a measured constraint.
  • 1 formats fired the degradation ladder. 1 dropped an element, and which one and why is on the record.
  • Contrast was measured line by line against the photograph each one ended up with, and the scrim was sized by compositing in sRGB.
One limit this engine does declare. 8 of the 66 sizes fall below 72 effective ppi: the master has 2160x2700 pixels and that does not stretch to a 3840x2160 canvas without upsampling. The engine measures it and says so on every piece. It does not fix it: asking for a higher-resolution source is the right answer, and inventing pixels would be worse than warning.
